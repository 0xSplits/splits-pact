PartyClanker — Crowd-Launched Vanilla Clanker

What this models: a crowd pools USDC, all-or-nothing, and launches a completely standard Clanker coin. 100% of the pool buys coins in the launch transaction itself (the "dev buy" — filled before any outside buyer can act). On top, a vault bonus reserves extra coins for the crowd: for every coin the pool bought, the vault streams more, over a longer window. Nobody is paid — no founder, no team; the organizer holds units like everyone else. The crowd's return: the coins (streamed), the bonus (streamed slower), and a cut of every trading fee, forever.

The crowd

All of it buys coins at launch. No founder slice, no LP slice — the buy itself puts the money into the trading pool.

The coin

The market cap the coin goes live at. Clanker's preset is ~$30K (10 ETH); going below that needs a custom pool config. Bigger list price = the pool's buy moves the price less.

The early-backer bonus

Bonus coins streamed per coin the pool bought. 1.0x = two coins for the price of one — the reward for committing before launch.

Fees

Every trade pays a 1% fee; Clanker keeps a fifth. With no founder, the crowd's units take the rest (any remainder here = platform share). Set permanently at launch.

Reality check: 86% of launches never see a single trade; only ~7% of funded launches ever repaid their seed via fees. Fee income is real only for coins a community keeps trading — which is why the crowd being the launcher is the whole idea.
Mechanics: the buy walks the launch curve (constant-product approximation against the coin's pool-side supply at list price), so its average fill is above list — the vault bonus pulls the crowd's blended price back down; both are shown. "Vs a launch-day buyer" compares the crowd's blended price to the pool price the instant after the crowd's buy — the best case any outside buyer sees. Why 50+ people matters: in 1.27M Base launches, coins with 50+ real, distinct day-one holders survived at far higher rates than money could buy — coins and units must land in each backer's own wallet. Mark-to-market cushion assumes the post-buy price, which selling would move. Arithmetic, not a prediction.
